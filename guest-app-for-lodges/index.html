A guest app that looks after your guests, before, during and after the stay.

A website can help win the booking. A guest companion can help look after the guest. I built a working demonstration for a bush lodge, and I can build one for yours.

It's shown here for a lodge, but the idea fits any business whose customers have questions before, during and after a visit: guesthouses, tour operators, venues, clinics, shops with orders to follow.

How it works for guests

Three steps, none of them hard.

  1. Get your link

    After you book, your host sends you a personal link.

  2. Open it on your phone

    It's a web app, so there is nothing to download. Add it to your home screen if you like.

  3. Take it into the bush

    Once opened, it keeps working without signal, which helps where reception is poor.

What a guest can do today

Everything here works in the demonstration.

  • Count down to arrival, with a short before-you-go checklist
  • Add flight details to their calendar as a reminder
  • See the lodge forecast, plus sunrise, sunset and the moon
  • Get directions and drive times, and find food, fuel, pharmacies and hospitals nearby
  • Find emergency numbers and message or call the host in one tap
  • Keep a wildlife passport with sightings, photos that stay on their phone, and small discovery badges
  • Make a trip memories card as a real image to share
  • Ask for extras such as a transfer or a braai pack

What it asks of the owner

A little effort at the start, then a link per booking.

The guest app runs on one shared engine, with one content file per lodge. Setting up a new lodge means changing content, not rebuilding the app.

Once, at the start
You confirm the details guests ask about: check-in and check-out, Wi-Fi, gate access and house rules. You choose your favourite places, set up the extras you offer and their prices, and approve the photos and wording.
For each booking
You create the guest's link with their name and dates, and send it. Today that's done in an owner screen. Later it could happen automatically from your booking system.
During stays
You reply to guests on WhatsApp, as you do now. Requests for extras and questions arrive there, already written out with the guest's name and dates.

What this could free up for an owner is the point of the idea, but I haven't measured it, so I won't put a number on it.

Questions lodge owners ask

Straight answers.

Do guests have to download anything?

No. After a booking, the host sends the guest a personal link. It opens in the phone's browser, and guests can add it to their home screen if they like.

Does it work without signal?

Once it has been opened, yes. That helps where reception is poor. The weather shows the last saved forecast and says so.

Does it connect to my booking system?

Not yet. In the demonstration, the host creates each guest's link by hand. Importing bookings automatically needs an integration with your booking system, which I'd scope with you.

Can guests send photos to the lodge?

Not today. Photos guests add to their wildlife passport stay on their phone and are private by default, and sharing is their choice. Letting guests upload photos to the lodge would need a backend and clear consent, and I'd discuss that with you.

Does it take payments?

No. Extras such as a transfer or a braai pack are requests. They open in WhatsApp or email, and the host confirms price and availability there.

Which languages does it support?

English and German. A language is only listed as supported when every screen is translated, and the safety and arrival wording in German should be checked by a native speaker before launch.

Is this only for lodges?

No. The idea works anywhere a customer has questions before, during and after a visit: guesthouses, tour operators, venues. If you're not sure it fits, tell me about your business and I'll say honestly whether it would help.
