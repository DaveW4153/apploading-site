01Data checking system

171,000 errors caught before they reached the new system.

Problem
A large company was moving to a new SAP system and needed its core business records checked first. No off-the-shelf tool covered the company's own rules, and nothing stopped bad records from going across.
Built

03LoadKit · in use today

One app, three clubs, 780 swimmers in 16 countries.

Problem
Open water swimmers in South Africa had no shared place to check water conditions and hazards, no easy way to organise group swims, and no way to prove a qualifying swim really happened.
Built
LoadKit, running as SwimLoading. Swimmers check live conditions, log swims with GPS, organise group swims, and compete in challenges and leaderboards. An Evidence Tracker records the details of a qualifying swim (duration, water temperature, GPS, observer contact) as it's logged, so nobody has to piece it together by hand afterwards. The same app runs two very different kinds of club: an adult open-water club with league racing, and pool squads with coaches, attendance and parents, including a foundation-funded learn-to-swim programme that reports progress straight to its funder.
Result
Self-funded. Zero marketing. Built and grown solo. A Maurten sponsorship followed.

04Ocean-swim safety and operations

Coordinating boats and crew, safely, from a phone at sea.

Problem
A Cape Town ocean-swim business was coordinating boats, crew and swimmers by phone and spreadsheet. There was no shared view of who was where, and no way for a family to know if a swimmer was safe mid-swim.
Built
A live operations system: swims are scheduled around weather windows, 11 kinds of incident can be logged, and an alert goes out automatically if a boat goes quiet. Families get a live tracking page that shows far more than a dot on a map: position against the planned route, pace and stroke-rate charts, water temperature, and a running feed of check-ins from the crew.
Result
Live since July 2026. Designed for cold, wet gloves on a moving deck, not for an office.

07Observer Hub · swim record verification

Six hours of paperwork saved after every swim.

Problem
For a long-distance swim to be officially recognised, an observer has to record what happened. That was usually written up by hand after the swim, then reviewed by an official body that had to take most of it on trust.
Built
Observer Hub: the observer opens a link on the boat, with nothing to install, and it keeps working without signal. Check-ins, feeds, attire and incidents are each stamped with the time and location. The official body has its own review area to question the observer, record its decision and issue the certificate.
Result
Saves the observer 6 hours of write-up after every swim. The record is locked the moment the swim ends, and nothing in it can be quietly changed: a correction is saved as a new version, with a reason.

Questions

Questions. Straight answers.

I only have an idea. Can we start there?

Yes, most projects start that way. Tell me what you want it to do and who it's for, in plain words. I work out what's needed with you, then quote for it.

Do I need to be technical?

No. You describe the problem. I make the technical decisions and explain the ones that matter in plain language.

What does it cost?

BuildTrack: R80,000-R120,000 setup + R6,000-R10,000 a month. LoadKit: R25,000-R60,000 setup + R3,000-R8,000 a month. Custom builds are quoted after a short call. Prices are in South African Rand; invoicing in USD/EUR is available for international clients.

How fast can we go live?

LoadKit in two weeks, BuildTrack in 4-6 weeks. Both are set up for you, not built from zero. Custom builds depend on size; most take 2-4 months.

Do you only build what's on this page?

No. The two products are ready-made, but most of the work shown here was built to order: data checking, internal operations systems, safety systems for people at sea. If your business needs software that has to work every day, it's in scope.

Who actually builds it?

One person: me. No account managers and no handoff between teams. You deal directly with the person doing the work, who still answers six months after launch.

What happens after launch?

I stay on. For a monthly fee I run it, fix what breaks and keep improving it, and you can still reach me directly.

Do you work with clients outside South Africa?

Yes. I'm based in Cape Town and work remotely across time zones. I reply the same business day.

What is a forward deployed engineer?

It's the name for how I work: inside your business rather than at a distance. I work out the real problem with your team, build against your own tools and information, make it solid enough for daily use, and stay on after launch.
